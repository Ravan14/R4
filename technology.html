Technology

Four sensors. One pass. One picture.

R4's satellites look at the same place at the same moment using radar, camera, heat, and radio sensors. Each sees what the others miss — and the platform turns their combined observations into answers decision-makers can act on. Detailed engineering stays confidential; the capability does not.

The system

Four ways of seeing, in a single pass.

Most satellites carry one sensor and see one thing. R4's formation carries four complementary sensors that observe the same area together. Each answers a different question — and the answers reinforce each other.

Camera — what it looks like

A camera in space shows what an area looks like — useful for identifying ships, buildings, and land use when the sky is clear and the sun is up.

Radar — what's there, in any weather

Radar (SAR) bounces radio waves off the ground, so it sees shapes and changes at night and through clouds. It cannot name an object — but it always sees that something is there.

Heat — what's active

A thermal sensor sees temperature, revealing fires, running engines, operating plants, and other energetic activity.

Radio — what it claims to be

Receivers read cooperative signals — a ship's identity beacon (AIS), an aircraft's transponder (ADS-B) — and compare what they claim against what the other sensors see.
